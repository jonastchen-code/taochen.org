Ecological Integrity of Avoided Deforestation Projects

Kelly Ong, Tao Chen, Zhimin Chen, Carlos T. Pérez-Brito and Yiwen Zeng

2026 · Published in Nature Climate Change 16(7): 769–773 · DOI 10.1038/s41558-026-02657-2

PDF not yet publicly available. The accepted manuscript in NTU’s DR-NTU repository is under embargo until 31 January 2027.

In one sentence

Most accredited avoided-deforestation carbon projects do not maintain the ecological condition of forests better than comparable unprotected areas.

Summary

Avoided-deforestation projects generate carbon credits by reducing forest loss, but it is unclear whether they also keep forests ecologically healthy. The paper compiles 196 accredited projects from major voluntary carbon registries and compares project areas with matched control areas in the same biome, excluding a 10-km zone around each project to allow for local leakage. For the 133 projects with good matches, it evaluates five indicators of ecological integrity: biodiversity intactness, forest landscape integrity, fragmentation, canopy height and net greenhouse-gas flux. Most projects show mixed, negligible or negative effects relative to controls, which points to shortcomings that limit these projects’ ability to safeguard ecosystems and sequester carbon.

Abstract as published

Reducing deforestation is proposed as a global climate action, yet it remains unclear whether carbon projects based on such interventions also maintain forests’ ecological conditions. Here we evaluate 133 projects against matched controls using five ecological-integrity indicators, to show that most projects have mixed, negligible or negative impacts relative to control areas. Results highlight fundamental shortcomings of these climate solutions that limit their ability to safeguard healthy ecosystems and sequester carbon.
