Analyst Coverage, Executive Compensation and Corporate Risk-Taking: Evidence from Property–Casualty Insurance Firms

Tao Chen, Shinichi Kamiya, Pingyi Lou and Andreas Milidonis

2023 · Published in Journal of Risk and Insurance 90(4): 899–939 · DOI 10.1111/jori.12437

PDF of Analyst Coverage, Executive Compensation and Corporate Risk-Taking: Evidence from Property–Casualty Insurance Firms (Accepted manuscript, February 2023)

In one sentence

When property–casualty insurers lose analyst coverage, they take more risk, especially where managers’ pay rewards risk-taking.

Summary

Using exogenous drops in analyst coverage caused by broker closures and mergers, the paper tests the causal effect of analyst coverage on risk-taking in an opaque industry, property–casualty insurance. After losing coverage, insurers’ risk increases on several book-based and market-based measures, including tail and default risk. The results are driven by firms whose managers have stronger risk-taking incentives in their compensation, and they are stronger for more opaque firms and firms with weaker policyholder monitoring. Risk rises through at least one risk-taking action, such as investing in higher-risk bonds.

Abstract as published

Using an exogenous drop in analyst coverage introduced by broker closures and mergers, we test for the causal impact of analyst coverage on corporate risk‐taking, in an opaque industry. We document an increase in risk using several book‐based and market‐based risk measures, including tail and default risk measures. Results are driven by firms with stronger managerial risk‐taking compensation incentives. The increase in risk is stronger in more opaque firms, and firms with weaker policyholder monitoring. Firm risk increases through at least one risk‐taking action, such as investing firm assets in higher‐risk bonds. Our study highlights the importance of stock analysts in affecting corporate risk‐taking, especially in the presence of stronger managerial, compensation risk‐taking incentives.
