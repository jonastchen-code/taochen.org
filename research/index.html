Sustainable Finance (12)

How environmental, social and governance forces shape firm behaviour: pollution and environmental policy, social responsibility, tax practices, and the oversight of managers.

  1. Ecological Integrity of Avoided Deforestation Projects

    Kelly Ong, Tao Chen, Zhimin Chen, Carlos T. Pérez-Brito and Yiwen Zeng

    Nature Climate Change 16(7), 2026

    Across 133 accredited avoided-deforestation carbon projects, most have mixed, negligible or negative effects on the ecological condition of forests relative to matched control areas.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Ecological Integrity of Avoided Deforestation Projects

  2. Minimum Wages, State Ownership, and Corporate Environmental Policies

    Tao Chen, Xi Xiong and Kunru Zou

    Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 60(6), 2025

    Minimum wage increases lead industrial firms to pollute more and reduce abatement, and state ownership mitigates these effects.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Minimum Wages, State Ownership, and Corporate Environmental Policies

  3. Labor Unionization and Real Earnings Management: Evidence from Labor Elections

    Vivek Astvansh, Beibei Wang, Tao Chen and Jimmy Chengyuan Qu

    PLOS ONE 19(2), 2024

    Firms whose workers narrowly vote to unionize reduce real earnings management relative to firms where the vote narrowly fails.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Labor Unionization and Real Earnings Management: Evidence from Labor Elections

  4. The Social Cost of Investor Distraction: Evidence from Institutional Cross-Blockholding

    Vivek Astvansh, Tao Chen and Jimmy Chengyuan Qu

    PLOS ONE 18(12), 2023

    Institutional investors who hold blocks in several firms in an industry are distracted, and the social performance of cross-held firms suffers.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for The Social Cost of Investor Distraction: Evidence from Institutional Cross-Blockholding

  5. Bank Deregulation and Corporate Environmental Performance

    Shiyi Chen, Tao Chen, Pingyi Lou, Hong Song and Chenyu Wu

    World Development 161, 2023

    Bank branching deregulation in China improved the environmental performance of more exposed firms, through technology upgrading and changes in asset mix.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Bank Deregulation and Corporate Environmental Performance

  6. The Unintended Consequence of Land Finance: Evidence from Corporate Tax Avoidance

    Tao Chen, Youchao Tan, Jinghua Wang and Cheng (Colin) Zeng

    Management Science 68(11), 2022

    When Chinese local governments lose land-sale revenue, they enforce taxes more strictly and firms in their jurisdiction avoid less tax.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for The Unintended Consequence of Land Finance: Evidence from Corporate Tax Avoidance

  7. Globalization and U.S. Corporate Tax Policies: Evidence from Import Competition

    Tao Chen, Chen Lin and Xiang Shao

    Management Science 68(8), 2022

    U.S. manufacturers more exposed to Chinese import competition after China received Permanent Normal Trade Relations increase their tax-reduction activities.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Globalization and U.S. Corporate Tax Policies: Evidence from Import Competition

  8. Does Credit Rating Conservatism Matter for Corporate Tax Avoidance?

    Tao Chen, Sidney Leung and Lingmin Xie

    Accounting & Finance 61(4), 2021

    More conservative credit ratings, following Dodd–Frank and the US sovereign downgrade, led firms to engage in more tax planning.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Does Credit Rating Conservatism Matter for Corporate Tax Avoidance?

  9. Institutional Shareholders and Corporate Social Responsibility

    Tao Chen, Hui Dong and Chen Lin

    Journal of Financial Economics 135(2), 2020

    Higher institutional ownership improves firms’ CSR performance, especially in financially material categories, partly through CSR-related shareholder proposals.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Institutional Shareholders and Corporate Social Responsibility

  10. Does Information Asymmetry Affect Corporate Tax Aggressiveness?

    Tao Chen and Chen Lin

    Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 52(5), 2017

    Firms avoid taxes more aggressively after an exogenous loss of analyst coverage, especially where tax-planning capacity is high and outside monitoring is weak.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Does Information Asymmetry Affect Corporate Tax Aggressiveness?

  11. Institutions, Board Structure, and Corporate Performance: Evidence from Chinese Firms

    Tao Chen

    Journal of Corporate Finance 32, 2015

    Examines how the institutional environment shapes the relation between board structure and the performance of Chinese listed firms.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Institutions, Board Structure, and Corporate Performance: Evidence from Chinese Firms

  12. Do Analysts Matter for Governance? Evidence from Natural Experiments

    Tao Chen, Jarrad Harford and Chen Lin

    Journal of Financial Economics 115(2), 2015

    After an exogenous loss of analyst coverage, CEOs receive higher excess pay, managers make more value-destroying acquisitions and manage earnings more.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Do Analysts Matter for Governance? Evidence from Natural Experiments

FinTech, AI, and Finance (6)

How financial technology and artificial intelligence change access to credit, innovation, and the way consumers respond to technology-driven markets.

  1. Cultural Lag, Consumer Literacy, and Coping in AI-Enabled Advertising: A Theoretical Explication and Managerial Solutions

    Chen Lou, Xuan Zhou, Elison Ai Ching Lim, Tao Chen, Edson C. Tandoc and Diotima Chattoraj

    Journal of Advertising 55(4), 2026

    Consumers often cannot tell AI-enabled ads from traditional ones without disclosure, and beliefs, literacy, public policy and education lag behind the technology.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Cultural Lag, Consumer Literacy, and Coping in AI-Enabled Advertising: A Theoretical Explication and Managerial Solutions

  2. What Influences Demand for Buy Now, Pay Later Credit?

    Tao Chen, Ben R. Marshall, Nhut H. Nguyen and Nuttawat Visaltanachoti

    Economics Letters 242, 2024

    In New Zealand, Buy Now, Pay Later demand is higher among single, younger and lower-income borrowers and falls as interest rates rise.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for What Influences Demand for Buy Now, Pay Later Credit?

  3. Authentically Fake? How Consumers Respond to the Influence of Virtual Influencers

    Chen Lou, Siu Ting Josie Kiew, Tao Chen, Tze Yen Michelle Lee, Jia En Celine Ong and ZhaoXi Phua

    Journal of Advertising 52(4), 2023

    Followers see virtual influencers as “authentically fake”: useful for brand awareness, but weak at driving purchase intention.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Authentically Fake? How Consumers Respond to the Influence of Virtual Influencers

  4. Finance and Firm Volatility: Evidence from Small Business Lending in China

    Tao Chen, Yi Huang, Chen Lin and Zixia Sheng

    Management Science 68(3), 2022

    Access to FinTech credit on Alibaba’s platform reduces sales volatility and exit risk for small online businesses.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Finance and Firm Volatility: Evidence from Small Business Lending in China

  5. Impact of Competition on Innovations of IT Industry: An Empirical Investigation

    Tao Chen, Hsing Kenneth Cheng, Yong (Jimmy) Jin, Shengli Li and Liangfei Qiu

    Journal of Management Information Systems 38(3), 2021

    Greater product market competition, identified through import tariff cuts, increases both the number and the quality of IT firms’ patents.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Impact of Competition on Innovations of IT Industry: An Empirical Investigation

  6. Financial Innovation: The Bright and the Dark Sides

    Thorsten Beck, Tao Chen, Chen Lin and Frank M. Song

    Journal of Banking & Finance 72, 2016

    Financial innovation is associated with faster growth in industries that rely on it, but also with greater bank fragility and larger losses during the 2007–08 crisis.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Financial Innovation: The Bright and the Dark Sides

Investors, Monitors and Corporate Policy (6)

How analysts, owners, regulators and governments shape corporate investment, risk-taking and firm value.

  1. Analyst Coverage, Executive Compensation and Corporate Risk-Taking: Evidence from Property–Casualty Insurance Firms

    Tao Chen, Shinichi Kamiya, Pingyi Lou and Andreas Milidonis

    Journal of Risk and Insurance 90(4), 2023

    An exogenous drop in analyst coverage raises risk-taking at property–casualty insurers, especially where managers have stronger risk-taking pay incentives.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Analyst Coverage, Executive Compensation and Corporate Risk-Taking: Evidence from Property–Casualty Insurance Firms

  2. Dual Ownership and Risk-Taking Incentives in Managerial Compensation

    Tao Chen, Li Zhang and Qifei Zhu

    Review of Finance 27(5), 2023

    Firms with more “dual holders”, institutions that hold both their equity and their bonds, give CEOs less risk-inducing pay: fewer stock options and more inside debt.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Dual Ownership and Risk-Taking Incentives in Managerial Compensation

  3. Tariff Uncertainty and Firm Innovation: Evidence from the U.S.–China Permanent Normal Trade Relation

    Tao Chen, Huasheng Gao and Yuxi Wang

    Journal of Empirical Finance 62, 2021

    Lower tariff uncertainty on Chinese imports after Permanent Normal Trade Relations led affected U.S. firms to patent more, with more highly cited patents.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Tariff Uncertainty and Firm Innovation: Evidence from the U.S.–China Permanent Normal Trade Relation

  4. Marginal Cost of Risk-Based Capital and Risk-Taking

    Tao Chen, Jing Rong Goh, Shinichi Kamiya and Pingyi Lou

    Journal of Banking & Finance 103, 2019

    Insurers facing lower marginal risk-based capital costs buy riskier fixed-income securities, an unintended consequence of the capital formula’s diversification benefit.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Marginal Cost of Risk-Based Capital and Risk-Taking

  5. Windfalls of Emperors' Sojourns: Stock Market Reactions to Chinese Firms Hosting High-Ranking Government Officials

    Douglas A. Schuler, Wei Shi, Robert E. Hoskisson and Tao Chen

    Strategic Management Journal 38(8), 2017

    Investors react positively when Chinese firms host visits by the President or Premier, most of all for private firms and firms with weaker prior performance.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for Windfalls of Emperors' Sojourns: Stock Market Reactions to Chinese Firms Hosting High-Ranking Government Officials

  6. How Does Analysts' Forecast Quality Relate to Corporate Investment Efficiency?

    Tao Chen, Lingmin Xie and Yuanyuan Zhang

    Journal of Corporate Finance 43, 2017

    Examines whether firms followed by analysts with higher-quality earnings forecasts invest more efficiently.

    Abstract and citation · Publisher version for How Does Analysts' Forecast Quality Relate to Corporate Investment Efficiency?
