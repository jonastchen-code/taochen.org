Sustainable Finance (12)

How environmental, social and governance forces shape firm behaviour: pollution and environmental policy, social responsibility, tax practices, and the oversight of managers.

  1. Ecological Integrity of Avoided Deforestation Projects

    Kelly Ong, Tao Chen, Zhimin Chen, Carlos T. Pérez-Brito and Yiwen Zeng

    2026 · Published in Nature Climate Change 16(7): 769–773

    In one sentence: Most accredited avoided-deforestation carbon projects do not maintain the ecological condition of forests better than comparable unprotected areas.

    Paper page: Ecological Integrity of Avoided Deforestation Projects · PDF not yet publicly available. The accepted manuscript in NTU’s DR-NTU repository is under embargo until 31 January 2027.

  2. Minimum Wages, State Ownership, and Corporate Environmental Policies

    Tao Chen, Xi Xiong and Kunru Zou

    2025 · Published in Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 60(6): 2921–2951

    In one sentence: Minimum wage increases lead industrial firms to pollute more and to cut abatement, and state ownership reduces these effects.

    Paper page: Minimum Wages, State Ownership, and Corporate Environmental Policies · PDF of Minimum Wages, State Ownership, and Corporate Environmental Policies (Published version, open access)

  3. Labor Unionization and Real Earnings Management: Evidence from Labor Elections

    Vivek Astvansh, Beibei Wang, Tao Chen and Jimmy Chengyuan Qu

    2024 · Published in PLOS ONE 19(2): e0292889

    In one sentence: Firms whose workers narrowly vote to unionize reduce real earnings management compared with firms where the vote narrowly fails.

    Paper page: Labor Unionization and Real Earnings Management: Evidence from Labor Elections · PDF of Labor Unionization and Real Earnings Management: Evidence from Labor Elections (Published version, open access)

  4. The Social Cost of Investor Distraction: Evidence from Institutional Cross-Blockholding

    Vivek Astvansh, Tao Chen and Jimmy Chengyuan Qu

    2023 · Published in PLOS ONE 18(12): e0286336

    In one sentence: When institutional investors hold blocks in several firms in an industry, they are distracted, and the social performance of those firms suffers.

    Paper page: The Social Cost of Investor Distraction: Evidence from Institutional Cross-Blockholding · PDF of The Social Cost of Investor Distraction: Evidence from Institutional Cross-Blockholding (Published version, open access)

  5. Bank Deregulation and Corporate Environmental Performance

    Shiyi Chen, Tao Chen, Pingyi Lou, Hong Song and Chenyu Wu

    2023 · Published in World Development 161: 106106

    In one sentence: After bank branching deregulation in China, firms more exposed to the deregulation emit less pollution per unit of output.

    Paper page: Bank Deregulation and Corporate Environmental Performance · PDF of Bank Deregulation and Corporate Environmental Performance (Accepted manuscript, November 2021)

  6. The Unintended Consequence of Land Finance: Evidence from Corporate Tax Avoidance

    Tao Chen, Youchao Tan, Jinghua Wang and Cheng (Colin) Zeng

    2022 · Published in Management Science 68(11): 8319–8342

    In one sentence: When Chinese local governments lose land-sale revenue, they enforce taxes more strictly and local firms avoid less tax.

    Paper page: The Unintended Consequence of Land Finance: Evidence from Corporate Tax Avoidance · PDF of The Unintended Consequence of Land Finance: Evidence from Corporate Tax Avoidance (Published version, open access)

  7. Globalization and U.S. Corporate Tax Policies: Evidence from Import Competition

    Tao Chen, Chen Lin and Xiang Shao

    2022 · Published in Management Science 68(8): 6145–6162

    In one sentence: U.S. manufacturers facing more competition from Chinese imports increase their tax-reduction activities.

    Paper page: Globalization and U.S. Corporate Tax Policies: Evidence from Import Competition · PDF of Globalization and U.S. Corporate Tax Policies: Evidence from Import Competition (Accepted manuscript, November 2020)

  8. Does Credit Rating Conservatism Matter for Corporate Tax Avoidance?

    Tao Chen, Sidney Leung and Lingmin Xie

    2021 · Published in Accounting & Finance 61(4): 5681–5730

    In one sentence: When credit rating agencies become more conservative, firms engage in more tax planning.

    Paper page: Does Credit Rating Conservatism Matter for Corporate Tax Avoidance? · PDF of Does Credit Rating Conservatism Matter for Corporate Tax Avoidance? (Working-paper version, SSRN)

  9. Institutional Shareholders and Corporate Social Responsibility

    Tao Chen, Hui Dong and Chen Lin

    2020 · Published in Journal of Financial Economics 135(2): 483–504

    In one sentence: Institutional shareholders improve the social and environmental performance of the firms they own.

    Paper page: Institutional Shareholders and Corporate Social Responsibility · PDF of Institutional Shareholders and Corporate Social Responsibility (Accepted manuscript, DR-NTU repository)

  10. Does Information Asymmetry Affect Corporate Tax Aggressiveness?

    Tao Chen and Chen Lin

    2017 · Published in Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 52(5): 2053–2081

    In one sentence: Firms avoid taxes more aggressively after they lose analyst coverage, which raises information asymmetry.

    Paper page: Does Information Asymmetry Affect Corporate Tax Aggressiveness? · PDF of Does Information Asymmetry Affect Corporate Tax Aggressiveness? (Published version, DR-NTU repository)

  11. Institutions, Board Structure, and Corporate Performance: Evidence from Chinese Firms

    Tao Chen

    2015 · Published in Journal of Corporate Finance 32: 217–237

    In one sentence: In China, weaker property-rights protection is associated with more outsiders on corporate boards, and in that environment outsiders improve firm performance.

    Paper page: Institutions, Board Structure, and Corporate Performance: Evidence from Chinese Firms · PDF of Institutions, Board Structure, and Corporate Performance: Evidence from Chinese Firms (Accepted manuscript, DR-NTU repository)

  12. Do Analysts Matter for Governance? Evidence from Natural Experiments

    Tao Chen, Jarrad Harford and Chen Lin

    2015 · Published in Journal of Financial Economics 115(2): 383–410

    In one sentence: Financial analysts act as monitors: when a firm loses analyst coverage, its managers behave in ways that are worse for outside shareholders.

    Paper page: Do Analysts Matter for Governance? Evidence from Natural Experiments · PDF of Do Analysts Matter for Governance? Evidence from Natural Experiments (Accepted manuscript, DR-NTU repository)

FinTech, AI, and Finance (6)

How financial technology and artificial intelligence change access to credit, innovation, and the way consumers respond to technology-driven markets.

  1. Cultural Lag, Consumer Literacy, and Coping in AI-Enabled Advertising: A Theoretical Explication and Managerial Solutions

    Chen Lou, Xuan Zhou, Elison Ai Ching Lim, Tao Chen, Edson C. Tandoc and Diotima Chattoraj

    2026 · Published in Journal of Advertising 55(4): 439–461

    In one sentence: Consumers’ beliefs, literacy, public policy and public education lag behind AI-enabled advertising, and most people cannot tell AI-enabled ads from traditional ones without disclosure.

    Paper page: Cultural Lag, Consumer Literacy, and Coping in AI-Enabled Advertising: A Theoretical Explication and Managerial Solutions · PDF of Cultural Lag, Consumer Literacy, and Coping in AI-Enabled Advertising: A Theoretical Explication and Managerial Solutions (Published version, open access)

  2. What Influences Demand for Buy Now, Pay Later Credit?

    Tao Chen, Ben R. Marshall, Nhut H. Nguyen and Nuttawat Visaltanachoti

    2024 · Published in Economics Letters 242: 111857

    In one sentence: In New Zealand, demand for Buy Now, Pay Later credit is higher among single, younger and lower-income people and falls when interest rates rise.

    Paper page: What Influences Demand for Buy Now, Pay Later Credit? · PDF of What Influences Demand for Buy Now, Pay Later Credit? (Published version, open access)

  3. Authentically Fake? How Consumers Respond to the Influence of Virtual Influencers

    Chen Lou, Siu Ting Josie Kiew, Tao Chen, Tze Yen Michelle Lee, Jia En Celine Ong and ZhaoXi Phua

    2023 · Published in Journal of Advertising 52(4): 540–557

    In one sentence: Followers see virtual influencers as uncanny but “authentically fake”: effective for brand awareness, but weak at persuading people to buy.

    Paper page: Authentically Fake? How Consumers Respond to the Influence of Virtual Influencers · PDF not yet publicly available. No public version found.

  4. Finance and Firm Volatility: Evidence from Small Business Lending in China

    Tao Chen, Yi Huang, Chen Lin and Zixia Sheng

    2022 · Published in Management Science 68(3): 2226–2249

    In one sentence: Access to FinTech credit on Alibaba’s platform makes small online businesses’ sales less volatile and makes them less likely to exit.

    Paper page: Finance and Firm Volatility: Evidence from Small Business Lending in China · PDF of Finance and Firm Volatility: Evidence from Small Business Lending in China (Accepted manuscript, October 2020)

  5. Impact of Competition on Innovations of IT Industry: An Empirical Investigation

    Tao Chen, Hsing Kenneth Cheng, Yong (Jimmy) Jin, Shengli Li and Liangfei Qiu

    2021 · Published in Journal of Management Information Systems 38(3): 647–666

    In one sentence: More product-market competition leads IT firms to produce more, and more highly cited, patents.

    Paper page: Impact of Competition on Innovations of IT Industry: An Empirical Investigation · PDF of Impact of Competition on Innovations of IT Industry: An Empirical Investigation (Accepted manuscript, April 2021)

  6. Financial Innovation: The Bright and the Dark Sides

    Thorsten Beck, Tao Chen, Chen Lin and Frank M. Song

    2016 · Published in Journal of Banking & Finance 72: 28–51

    In one sentence: Financial innovation is associated with faster bank growth but also with greater bank fragility, and on net with higher economic growth where growth opportunities are better.

    Paper page: Financial Innovation: The Bright and the Dark Sides · PDF of Financial Innovation: The Bright and the Dark Sides (Accepted manuscript, DR-NTU repository)

Investors, Monitors and Corporate Policy (6)

How analysts, owners, regulators and governments shape corporate investment, risk-taking and firm value.

  1. Analyst Coverage, Executive Compensation and Corporate Risk-Taking: Evidence from Property–Casualty Insurance Firms

    Tao Chen, Shinichi Kamiya, Pingyi Lou and Andreas Milidonis

    2023 · Published in Journal of Risk and Insurance 90(4): 899–939

    In one sentence: When property–casualty insurers lose analyst coverage, they take more risk, especially where managers’ pay rewards risk-taking.

    Paper page: Analyst Coverage, Executive Compensation and Corporate Risk-Taking: Evidence from Property–Casualty Insurance Firms · PDF of Analyst Coverage, Executive Compensation and Corporate Risk-Taking: Evidence from Property–Casualty Insurance Firms (Accepted manuscript, February 2023)

  2. Dual Ownership and Risk-Taking Incentives in Managerial Compensation

    Tao Chen, Li Zhang and Qifei Zhu

    2023 · Published in Review of Finance 27(5): 1823–1857

    In one sentence: When more of a firm is owned by institutions that hold both its shares and its bonds, CEO pay is designed to encourage less risk-taking.

    Paper page: Dual Ownership and Risk-Taking Incentives in Managerial Compensation · PDF of Dual Ownership and Risk-Taking Incentives in Managerial Compensation (Accepted manuscript, March 2023)

  3. Tariff Uncertainty and Firm Innovation: Evidence from the U.S.–China Permanent Normal Trade Relation

    Tao Chen, Huasheng Gao and Yuxi Wang

    2021 · Published in Journal of Empirical Finance 62: 12–27

    In one sentence: Lower uncertainty about future tariffs on Chinese imports led affected U.S. firms to innovate more.

    Paper page: Tariff Uncertainty and Firm Innovation: Evidence from the U.S.–China Permanent Normal Trade Relation · PDF of Tariff Uncertainty and Firm Innovation: Evidence from the U.S.–China Permanent Normal Trade Relation (Accepted manuscript, November 2020)

  4. Marginal Cost of Risk-Based Capital and Risk-Taking

    Tao Chen, Jing Rong Goh, Shinichi Kamiya and Pingyi Lou

    2019 · Published in Journal of Banking & Finance 103: 130–145

    In one sentence: A diversification feature of risk-based capital rules leads insurers with lower marginal capital costs to buy riskier bonds.

    Paper page: Marginal Cost of Risk-Based Capital and Risk-Taking · PDF of Marginal Cost of Risk-Based Capital and Risk-Taking (Accepted manuscript, DR-NTU repository)

  5. Windfalls of Emperors' Sojourns: Stock Market Reactions to Chinese Firms Hosting High-Ranking Government Officials

    Douglas A. Schuler, Wei Shi, Robert E. Hoskisson and Tao Chen

    2017 · Published in Strategic Management Journal 38(8): 1668–1687

    In one sentence: Investors react positively when Chinese firms host visits by the President or Premier, especially private firms and firms with weaker prior performance.

    Paper page: Windfalls of Emperors' Sojourns: Stock Market Reactions to Chinese Firms Hosting High-Ranking Government Officials · PDF of Windfalls of Emperors' Sojourns: Stock Market Reactions to Chinese Firms Hosting High-Ranking Government Officials (Accepted manuscript, DR-NTU repository)

  6. How Does Analysts' Forecast Quality Relate to Corporate Investment Efficiency?

    Tao Chen, Lingmin Xie and Yuanyuan Zhang

    2017 · Published in Journal of Corporate Finance 43: 217–240

    In one sentence: Higher-quality analyst forecasts are associated with more efficient corporate investment, reducing both under- and over-investment.

    Paper page: How Does Analysts' Forecast Quality Relate to Corporate Investment Efficiency? · PDF of How Does Analysts' Forecast Quality Relate to Corporate Investment Efficiency? (Accepted manuscript, DR-NTU repository)
